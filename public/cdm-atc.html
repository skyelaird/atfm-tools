Working a CDM airport with atfm-tools

For aerodrome controllers (DEL / GND / TWR) at a Canadian scope airport while atfm-tools is issuing CTOTs. Read top to bottom — it follows one departure from connection to wheels-up. Verified against rpuig2001/CDM at cc5a2bc.

The short version. We compute the slot; the plugin puts it in your tag as a TSAT; you approve start-up against it. You are the delivery channel — nothing we calculate reaches a pilot unless you pass it on, or point them at the portal.

Before you start — once per session

  1. Connect on a controlling position. The plugin refuses to act for OBS and other non-active positions. It accepts _DEL, _GND, _TWR, _APP, _CTR and _FMP.
  2. Point the CTOT feed at us. In CDMconfig.xml, set <customRestricted url="https://atfm.momentaryshutter.com/cdm/etfms/restricted"/> and leave <viffSystem> at its default. That override swaps only CTOT delivery — rates, relevant flights and everything else still come from vIFF.
  3. Set the pilot message. <CDM><PrivateMessage text="…"/>. Out of the box the plugin sends pilots to vats.im/vdgs, which is VATSIM Spain's panel and knows nothing about our slots. Point it at our pilot portal instead.
  4. Claim the airport: .cdm master CYYZ. Until you do, the plugin holds no flights for it and you will see nothing. .cdm help lists the rest (lvo, realmode, server, remarks, rate).

The thread — one departure

1. The flight appears

Pilot connects and files. We derive TOBT from the filed EOBT, or from spawn + 20 min when the EOBT is already stale — which on VATSIM it usually is. TSAT and TTOT cascade from there. Nothing is regulated yet; the tag shows times, not a slot.

2. A slot arrives — only if a regulation is active

When the FMP has a regulation running at the destination, our allocator issues a CTOT for this departure. The plugin polls us about every 15 s per master airport, so it lands in your CTOT tag item within a quarter of a minute of being issued.

No regulation means no CTOT, and that is the normal state. An empty CTOT column is not a fault.

3. Read the tag

ItemWhat it is
EOBTWhat the pilot filed. Treat as a rumour.
TOBTWhen the aircraft is actually expected to be ready. The number that matters.
TSATStart-up approval time. This is what you issue against.
TTOTTarget take-off = TOBT + taxi.
CTOTOur slot, when one exists. TSAT is then derived backwards from it.
Network StsWhere the flight sits in the CDM sequence.

Colours do the nagging for you: TSAT flashes orange in its first minute and turns red once it is stale; TTOT goes red past TTOT + 10; CTOT shows yellow when the flight is flagged REA.

4. Pilot calls ready

Use Ready TOBT on the TOBT item. If they are clearly not ready — still boarding, no pushback crew — Edit TOBT forward instead. Everything downstream re-cascades, and if the flight is regulated we recompute the CTOT on the next allocator run (≤ 2 min).

Moving TOBT earlier than the aircraft can actually make it is the one change that reliably wastes a slot. An optimistic TOBT buys a slot the aircraft then misses, and the next flight cannot have it back.

5. Approve start-up at TSAT

Issue start-up against TSAT, then set the EuroScope ground state to STUP. That is what stamps ASAT and pushes it back through the plugin.

ASAT exists only because you set that ground state. We never invent it — there is no position signal for "start-up approved", so an unset ground state means the milestone simply does not exist in the record.

6. Push and taxi

Ground states PUSH then TAXI as normal. Separately, we observe AOBT ourselves from the aircraft's own movement — the first report with groundspeed above zero at the stand. The two can disagree by a minute or two: ours feeds taxi-time statistics, yours drives the CDM sequence. Neither is wrong.

7. Take-off

We stamp ATOT from the position feed and measure it against the CTOT. Compliance shows up on the FMP's reports page, not yours.

8. If the slot slips

With one of our CTOTs attached, the plugin stops running its own departure sequencing for that flight — it takes our time as given. If its computed take-off drifts past the CTOT but stays inside CTOT + 7 min, it quietly adjusts. Past that the slot is missed: ask the FMP for a new one, or set a manual CTOT if you need to move the aircraft now.

Actions on the CTOT item

ActionUse it when
Set Manual CTOTYou need a specific take-off time for this flight regardless of what we said. Local to your session.

Disable CDM-NetworkTake one flight out of the automated flow entirely. We keep issuing; the plugin stops applying.
Get FM as textYou want the flow reason in words, to read to a pilot.
Send VDGS PMPrivate-messages the pilot with your configured text. The cheapest way to get an unresponsive pilot watching their own times.

What outranks what

ECFMP restriction > Disable CDM-Network > our CTOT > event slot > plugin's own sequencing

If a flight carries an ECFMP restriction the plugin skips our CTOT for it completely. That is correct — the European measure is a real constraint and we are not modelling it.

Four things that surprise people

  1. Our list is authoritative on every poll. The plugin wipes all CTOTs and re-applies whatever we just sent. A callsign we omit is a callsign whose CTOT is released — that is how a lifted regulation clears your screen within 15 s.
  2. An outage is safer than a bad response. If our server errors, the plugin logs it and keeps the CTOTs you already have. Only a valid-but-wrong response can clear them.
  3. Rates come from two places. The plugin merges its own rate.txt for departure sequencing, but the arrival rate that shaped your CTOTs is the one the FMP declared on our AAR page. If they disagree, the CTOT already reflects ours.
  4. No ATC at the departure airport is the normal case. Most regulated flights push without anyone to read them a TSAT. That is what the pilot portal is for — a pilot enters their callsign and reads their own TOBT, TSAT and CTOT. Send them there early rather than explaining a slot over text.
Still unproven. This round trip has not yet been exercised against a live EuroScope session. Until it has, treat a CTOT that fails to appear as a bug worth reporting rather than as pilot error — and tell the FMP what you saw.
